# Phase 3 — Measurement and Psychometric Validation Protocol (pre-fieldwork draft)

## Construct map and boundary

- **Philosophy (ph1–ph4):** Reflective, dialogic, learner-centred *teaching orientation*. It does **not** represent the whole of educational philosophy, philosophical knowledge, or teacher personality.
- **Digital literacy (dl1–dl4):** Self-reported **pedagogical digital-literacy practices**; not a performance test of competence. Inspiration: UNESCO ICT-CFT v3 (2018) and European Commission DigCompEdu (2017); **all items are newly authored**, not copied/adapted validated inventory items.
- **Equity (eq1–eq4):** Self-reported inclusive practices and resource-sensitive decision-making; not an objective equity-outcome index.

Each item is scored 1=Strongly disagree, 2=Disagree, 3=Neither, 4=Agree, 5=Strongly agree; no reverse-keyed items in this draft. Each subscale mean is computed only with all four responses. No diagnostic cut-offs, no individual ranking. Responses are ordinal, so robust ordinal methods are preferred for final construct analysis.

## Evidence needed BEFORE describing scales as validated

1. Expert review by 5–8 specialists covering educational philosophy, educational/developmental psychology, secondary teacher education, inclusive education, measurement and local Bangla language; document item-level relevance, clarity, content validity index and divergent opinions. Experts should not be claimed to have approved this draft.
2. Independent Bangla translation review, reconciliation and back-translation; cognitive interviews with ~8–15 teachers representing divisions, urban/rural schools and varying ICT access; explicitly revise ambiguous Bengali phrasing.
3. Ethics approval; plan recruitment using school-level strata (e.g. division, urban/rural, school type). Record recruitment counts and nonresponse; avoid claiming national representativeness from convenience sampling. Teachers are clustered within schools.
4. Pilot (~30–50) for feasibility, time burden, item distributions, response-process evidence and preliminary internal consistency. These are pragmatic planning ranges, not universal sample-size rules.
5. For field validation, preregister sample-size justification based on model complexity, clustering and expected precision; a larger independent sample is normally necessary for factor analysis. Use ordinal/polychoric EFA and separate-sample CFA when feasible; report fit, factor loadings, residuals, reliability (omega with confidence intervals; alpha as supplementary), convergent/discriminant evidence and group invariance only when appropriately powered.
6. Consider classroom observation or teaching artefacts and technology access as triangulation to address common-method bias. Evaluate fairness across gender, setting, disability inclusion and resource access while avoiding tiny subgroup disclosures.
7. Longitudinal/cross-sectional associations do **not** imply causal effects. World Bank national indicators are contextual—not row-level predictors for individual teachers without justified linkage.

## Predefined descriptive analyses

Report counts, response coverage, item distributions, subscale mean/SD only with complete item blocks; alpha is exploratory; correlations are associations, not construct validity proof. Do not interpolate missing survey items. For nonresponse, report denominators transparently. Protect small-cell confidentiality using institutional disclosure rules.

## Data governance

Store participant datasets **outside the public repository**, with restricted access. Do not collect names, phone, emails, student information, GPS, IP or exact school identifier in public exports. Reserve consent logs separately from responses. Repository test/demo CSV is synthetic and cannot support substantive psychology findings.
